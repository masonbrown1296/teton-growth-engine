## Headline

## Subhead
Teton gives senior living care teams continuous visibility into resident health patterns, so they can act early instead of reacting late.

## Section 1: The Problem

Most senior living operators are flying blind between scheduled check-ins. Staff learn about declining mobility, disrupted sleep, or behavioral changes only after a fall, an ER visit, or a family complaint. The tools available today are reactive by design: they detect events that have already happened, then send an alert. Meanwhile, the patterns that preceded those events were visible for days or weeks, and nobody had a way to see them.

## Section 2: How Teton Works

Teton installs a small sensor and compute unit in resident rooms that continuously interprets activity and health signals, including mobility, sleep quality, respiration, and staff interactions. All AI processing happens on the device itself. No video ever leaves the room, and residents wear nothing. Care teams receive alerts through the Teton app when patterns shift: declining mobility, disrupted sleep, changes in overnight respiration. Your Tuesday morning check-in catches something that would have been Saturday's ER visit.

## Section 3: Proof

**74% fewer falls at Sagora Senior Living.** Sagora, a Texas-based operator, deployed Teton across two communities and saw falls drop by nearly three-quarters, with a 95% improvement in response time to the incidents that did occur.

**99.8% resident and family adoption at Aquinas Senior Living.** Aquinas rolled out Teton at Heritage Springs in Pennsylvania, then expanded to Wynwood House. Near-universal adoption from residents and families, with zero wearables to manage and zero privacy concerns to navigate.
